\noindent Dear Editors,

\medskip
We submit the manuscript ``Exact exponential heat rectification from the Liouvillian skin effect'' by Hikaru Wakaura and Taiki Tanimae for consideration as a Letter.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Claim.} A reciprocal tight-binding chain with directional bond dissipation rectifies heat with a ratio that obeys an exact law, $\delta Q_f/\delta Q_r=\exp[2(L-1)/\xi]$, where $\xi$ is the Liouvillian skin localisation length; the law is independent of energy, temperature and on-site disorder, and a 40-site chain reaches 99\% rectification.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Why Physical Review Letters.} Thermal diodes have so far required nonlinearity or structural asymmetry and rectify by at most a factor of ten, independent of device size. Our result shows that dissipation alone rectifies heat, that the ratio grows exponentially with length, and that one number controls it. The two works that define the Liouvillian skin effect, Song, Yao and Wang [Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\ \textbf{123}, 170401 (2019)] and Haga, Nakagawa, Hamazaki and Ueda [Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\ \textbf{127}, 070402 (2021)], and the cold-atom observation of Liang \textit{et al.} [Phys.\ Rev.\ Lett.\ \textbf{129}, 070401 (2022)] appeared in this journal; our Letter answers the transport question they left open, with three further results of general interest: the controlling quantity beyond nearest neighbours is the non-Bloch localisation length and not the spectral winding number, interactions up to $2J$ do not renormalise $\xi$ (matrix-product-state simulations calibrated against the exact free-fermion solution), and a finite-temperature bath closes the entropy balance with a cost per rectified heat that grows as $1/\xi$. The proposal is within reach of momentum-lattice condensates and superconducting circuits with engineered loss.
