Directing heat is as basic to thermal engineering as directing charge is to electronics, yet every thermal diode to date relies on nonlinearity or structural asymmetry. Here we show that a reciprocal tight-binding chain with directional bond dissipation rectifies heat with the exact ratio $\delta Q_f/\delta Q_r=\exp[2(L-1)/\xi]$, where $L$ is the chain length and $\xi$ the Liouvillian skin localisation length, so that forty sites suffice for 99% rectification. The law holds at every energy and temperature and for arbitrary on-site disorder; beyond nearest neighbours the exponent is the non-Bloch localisation length, not the spectral winding number, and matrix-product-state simulations show that interactions up to twice the hopping leave the skin length unrenormalised. A thermal bath in place of the loss closes the entropy balance at a cost per rectified heat that grows with the inverse skin length. Our results make the Liouvillian skin effect a design principle for size-scalable heat diodes in cold atoms and superconducting circuits.
